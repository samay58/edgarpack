# SaaS Evolution, Through Filings

This memo is built from `reports/saas-evolution` evidence tables. Company operating claims cite SEC-derived EdgarPack rows. Market-regime and current AI-packaging claims are separately labeled as external context because filings do not reliably expose current price cards, credit allowances, or public-market multiple regimes.

## Thesis

The last decade did not turn SaaS into one thing. It split the model into at least five economically different software businesses:

- Contracted enterprise suites, where RPO/cRPO and renewal discipline matter as much as reported revenue. Salesforce had fiscal 2025 revenue of $41.525 billion, 20.1% operating margin, 34.7% FCF margin, $63.4 billion of RPO, and $30.2 billion of cRPO. ServiceNow had fiscal 2025 revenue of $13.278 billion, 13.7% operating margin, and 34.5% FCF margin; its filing defines RPO/cRPO as contracted backlog measures rather than ARR. [standard-financials.csv:CRM-current; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:CRM-latest_disclosed-remaining-performance-obligation; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:CRM-latest_disclosed-current-remaining-performance-obligation; standard-financials.csv:NOW-current; metric-definition-table.csv:NOW-rpo-and-crpo-definition]
- Subscription suites that have already crossed the license-to-cloud transition. Adobe had fiscal 2025 revenue of $23.769 billion, 36.6% operating margin, 41.4% FCF margin, Total Adobe ARR of $25.20 billion, and Digital Media ARR of $19.20 billion. [standard-financials.csv:ADBE-current; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:ADBE-latest_disclosed-total-adobe-arr; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:ADBE-latest_disclosed-digital-media-arr; metric-definition-table.csv:ADBE-adobe-arr-definition]
- Consumption and usage platforms, where the accounting line is subscription revenue but investor underwriting has to follow usage, product breadth, and metric-definition caveats. Snowflake had fiscal 2026 revenue of $4.684 billion, -30.6% operating margin, 23.9% FCF margin, 13,328 customers, 733 customers over $1 million in trailing-12-month product revenue, and 125% net revenue retention. MongoDB had fiscal 2026 revenue of $2.464 billion, -5.6% operating margin, 20.3% FCF margin, 65,200 customers, and 121% net ARR expansion. Datadog had fiscal 2025 revenue of $3.427 billion, 29.2% FCF margin, and 32,700 customers. [standard-financials.csv:SNOW-current; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:SNOW-latest_disclosed-total-customers; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:SNOW-latest_disclosed-customers-that-contributed-more-than--1-million-in-trailing-12-month-product-revenue; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:SNOW-latest_disclosed-net-revenue-retention-rate; metric-definition-table.csv:SNOW-nrr-and-rpo-caveat-definition; standard-financials.csv:MDB-current; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:MDB-latest_disclosed-customers; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:MDB-latest_disclosed-net-annualized-recurring-revenue---arr---expansion-rate; metric-definition-table.csv:MDB-atlas-arr-definition; standard-financials.csv:DDOG-current; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:DDOG-latest_disclosed-customers]
- Transaction-embedded software, where GMV and payment penetration are central to monetization. Shopify had fiscal 2025 revenue of $11.556 billion, 12.7% operating margin, 17.4% FCF margin, $248.1 billion of GMV facilitated by Shopify Payments, and 65.6% Shopify Payments penetration. [standard-financials.csv:SHOP-current; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:SHOP-latest_disclosed-gmv-that-was-facilitated-using-shopify-payments; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:SHOP-latest_disclosed-shopify-payments-penetration-rate; business-model-table.csv:SHOP-current-model]
- Seat-based collaboration and SMB platforms, where customer count, ARPC, app ecosystems, and seat expansion have to be read together. Zoom had fiscal 2026 revenue of $4.869 billion, 23.1% operating margin, 39.5% FCF margin, 7,438 full-time employees, and more than 3,100 apps/integrations. HubSpot had fiscal 2025 revenue of $3.131 billion, 22.6% FCF margin, 288,706 customers, and $11,414 average subscription revenue per customer. Atlassian had fiscal 2025 revenue of $5.215 billion, 27.1% FCF margin, more than 300,000 customers, and over $1.1 billion of Marketplace gross purchases in fiscal 2024. [standard-financials.csv:ZM-current; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:ZM-latest_disclosed-full-time-employees; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:ZM-latest_disclosed-integrations; standard-financials.csv:HUBS-current; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:HUBS-latest_disclosed-customers; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:HUBS-latest_disclosed-average-subscription-revenue-per-customer; standard-financials.csv:TEAM-current; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:TEAM-latest_disclosed-customers; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:TEAM-latest_disclosed-purchases-of-third-party-apps]

The underwriting problem is no longer "what is ARR?" It is "what kind of software economic machine is this, and which disclosed metric is the least distorted proxy for forward demand?" That distinction is visible in the filings: Salesforce and ServiceNow push investors toward contracted backlog, Adobe toward ARR, Snowflake and MongoDB toward usage-sensitive retention/ARR constructs, Shopify toward payments GMV, and Workday toward subscription backlog. ## What Changed

### 1. Scale became less informative without model type.

The current cohort spans $41.525 billion of revenue at Salesforce, $23.769 billion at Adobe, $13.278 billion at ServiceNow, $11.556 billion at Shopify, $9.552 billion at Workday, $5.215 billion at Atlassian, $4.869 billion at Zoom, $4.684 billion at Snowflake, $3.427 billion at Datadog, $3.131 billion at HubSpot, and $2.464 billion at MongoDB. [standard-financials.csv:CRM-current; standard-financials.csv:ADBE-current; standard-financials.csv:NOW-current; standard-financials.csv:SHOP-current; standard-financials.csv:WDAY-current; standard-financials.csv:TEAM-current; standard-financials.csv:ZM-current; standard-financials.csv:SNOW-current; standard-financials.csv:DDOG-current; standard-financials.csv:HUBS-current; standard-financials.csv:MDB-current]

But the same revenue line means different things. Shopify explicitly combines subscription solutions with merchant solutions. Snowflake calls out a consumption-based business model and warns that RPO excludes on-demand arrangements without minimum commitments. MongoDB annualizes Atlas usage differently for direct-sales and self-serve channels. Workday's forward signal is subscription revenue backlog. [business-model-table.csv:SHOP-current-model; business-model-table.csv:SNOW-current-model; metric-definition-table.csv:SNOW-nrr-and-rpo-caveat-definition; metric-definition-table.csv:MDB-atlas-arr-definition; business-model-table.csv:WDAY-current-model; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:WDAY-latest_disclosed-total-subscription-revenue-backlog]

Investor implication: cross-company revenue multiples are lazy unless the analyst normalizes for how demand enters the income statement. A usage business with weak RPO can still have strong latent consumption; a backlog-heavy suite can look safer while exposing investors to duration, renewal, and discounting artifacts. [metric-definition-table.csv:SNOW-nrr-and-rpo-caveat-definition; metric-definition-table.csv:CRM-rpo-and-crpo-definition; metric-definition-table.csv:NOW-rpo-and-crpo-definition]

### 2. The market forced a profitability regime change.

The filing evidence shows mature SaaS is now expected to show real cash economics: Salesforce reported 34.7% FCF margin in fiscal 2025, ServiceNow 34.5%, Adobe 41.4%, Zoom 39.5%, Workday 29.1%, Datadog 29.2%, Atlassian 27.1%, HubSpot 22.6%, MongoDB 20.3%, Snowflake 23.9%, and Shopify 17.4%. [standard-financials.csv:CRM-current; standard-financials.csv:NOW-current; standard-financials.csv:ADBE-current; standard-financials.csv:ZM-current; standard-financials.csv:WDAY-current; standard-financials.csv:DDOG-current; standard-financials.csv:TEAM-current; standard-financials.csv:HUBS-current; standard-financials.csv:MDB-current; standard-financials.csv:SNOW-current; standard-financials.csv:SHOP-current]

External market context explains why this mattered after 2021. Bessemer describes public cloud market capitalization peaking at $2.7 trillion in November 2021 and falling to roughly $1.4 trillion by May 2022. OpenView cites fast-growing public SaaS valuations falling from 46.2x to 11.0x in the prior year and notes that customers delayed purchases, lengthened deal timelines, and scrutinized cloud spend in 2023. Meritech's March 2024 pulse says forward growth rates fell from 48% in 2022 to 27% YTD while FCF margins rose. [valuation-context-table.csv:valuation-bessemer-state-of-the-cloud-2022-2021-peak-to-2022-reset; valuation-context-table.csv:valuation-openview-2023-saas-benchmarks-post-zirp-growth-compression; valuation-context-table.csv:valuation-meritech-software-pulse-efficiency-era-public-saas]

Investor implication: the old "growth at any cost" shorthand is no longer an adequate public SaaS framework. The better question is whether the company has a durable metric spine after sales efficiency normalizes: backlog for Salesforce/ServiceNow/Workday, ARR for Adobe, usage retention for Snowflake/MongoDB, payments GMV for Shopify, customer monetization for HubSpot. [kpi-disclosure-table.csv:CRM-latest_disclosed-remaining-performance-obligation; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:NOW-latest_disclosed-customers; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:WDAY-latest_disclosed-total-subscription-revenue-backlog; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:ADBE-latest_disclosed-total-adobe-arr; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:SNOW-latest_disclosed-net-revenue-retention-rate; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:MDB-latest_disclosed-net-annualized-recurring-revenue---arr---expansion-rate; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:SHOP-latest_disclosed-gmv-that-was-facilitated-using-shopify-payments; kpi-disclosure-table.csv:HUBS-latest_disclosed-average-subscription-revenue-per-customer]

### 3. AI is becoming packaging, not just product copy.

This section uses external sources because filings do not expose live AI price cards or credit rules. Salesforce's 2025 pricing update says Agentforce add-ons start at $125 per user per month, Agentforce 1 Editions start at $550 per user per month, and Agentforce 1 bundles 1 million Flex Credits plus 2.5 million Data Services Credits per org per year. ServiceNow's Q4 2024 release says AI Agents will be part of Pro Plus and Enterprise Plus and that 2025 would shift more AI/data monetization toward consumption-based elements. Adobe meters Firefly through generative credits and sells credit add-ons. Atlassian measures Rovo usage in pooled monthly credits per seat, with 10 credits/request for Rovo Chat or Agents and 100 credits/request for Deep Research. GitLab announced Duo Pro at $19 per user per month. [ai-packaging-table.csv:CRM-ai-packaging-agentforce-add-ons-and-agentforce-1-editions; ai-packaging-table.csv:NOW-ai-packaging-now-assist-ai-agents-ai-agent-orchestrator-ai-agent-studio; ai-packaging-table.csv:ADBE-ai-packaging-adobe-firefly-generative-ai-plans-and-credit-add-ons; ai-packaging-table.csv:TEAM-ai-packaging-rovo-and-rovo-dev; ai-packaging-table.csv:GTLB-ai-packaging-gitlab-duo-pro]

The AI packaging pattern is not uniform. Salesforce is combining premium seats with credits; ServiceNow is putting agents into higher SKUs while pointing toward consumption; Adobe and Atlassian expose credit systems; GitLab's cited announcement is a straightforward per-user add-on. [ai-packaging-table.csv:CRM-ai-packaging-agentforce-add-ons-and-agentforce-1-editions; ai-packaging-table.csv:NOW-ai-packaging-now-assist-ai-agents-ai-agent-orchestrator-ai-agent-studio; ai-packaging-table.csv:ADBE-ai-packaging-adobe-firefly-generative-ai-plans-and-credit-add-ons; ai-packaging-table.csv:TEAM-ai-packaging-rovo-and-rovo-dev; ai-packaging-table.csv:GTLB-ai-packaging-gitlab-duo-pro]

Investor implication: AI monetization will make SaaS comparability worse before it makes it better. The relevant unit can be a seat, credit, request, data-service credit, workflow, displaced-labor proxy, or bundled entitlement. OpenView's 2023 benchmark says AI positioning alone was not enough, while companies that launched and monetized AI products were more likely to be outliers; Battery's 2024 OpenCloud commentary recommends value-based AI pricing across per-seat, consumption-based, or displaced-labor models. [valuation-context-table.csv:valuation-openview-2023-saas-benchmarks-ai-monetization-threshold; valuation-context-table.csv:valuation-battery-opencloud-2024-ai-supercycle-monetization-design]

## What EdgarPack Proved

EdgarPack was strongest when the work had to stay inside primary documents. It gave this bundle a real evidence spine: 29 standard-financial rows, 30 KPI disclosure rows, 11 business-model rows, 6 metric-definition rows, 5 AI-packaging rows, and 6 valuation-context rows, all mapped into `source-ledger.csv`. [standard-financials.csv; kpi-disclosure-table.csv; business-model-table.csv; metric-definition-table.csv; ai-packaging-table.csv; valuation-context-table.csv; source-ledger.csv]

The important unlock is not "it can summarize filings." The unlock is that an investor can build a cohort-level operating map without manually opening every 10-K, while preserving enough provenance to audit the claim later. That mattered most for non-obvious metric definitions: MongoDB's Atlas ARR annualizes recent usage; Snowflake's RPO excludes on-demand arrangements without minimum commitments; Salesforce and ServiceNow RPO/cRPO are contracted backlog measures, not clean ARR; Adobe's ARR framing changed in the current disclosure. [metric-definition-table.csv:MDB-atlas-arr-definition; metric-definition-table.csv:SNOW-nrr-and-rpo-caveat-definition; metric-definition-table.csv:CRM-rpo-and-crpo-definition; metric-definition-table.csv:NOW-rpo-and-crpo-definition; metric-definition-table.csv:ADBE-adobe-arr-definition]

The proof-of-concept also exposed exactly where EdgarPack should be hardened for real investor use:

- Filing discovery needs an investor annual mode. SHOP, TEAM, and other non-standard filers require coherent 10-K / 20-F / 40-F / S-1 / F-1 discovery rather than a one-form happy path. [filing-selection-notes.md; edgarpack-friction-log.md]
- Query period semantics need safety rails. The original `lfy-10` query path could produce a recent nearest-available year that looked like a baseline; the research bundle only became reliable after switching to explicit annual-series rows and marking earliest/closest years as caveated. [search-notes/standard-financials.md; standard-financials.csv:CRM-baseline; standard-financials.csv:SHOP-baseline; standard-financials.csv:TEAM-baseline]
- `which` needs machine-grade JSON stdout and stronger batch behavior. The workflow had to strip progress logs from JSON output and recover from slow or stalled KPI discovery runs. [edgarpack-friction-log.md; raw/edgarpack/which-SHOP.json; raw/edgarpack/which-TEAM.json]
- SaaS metric discovery should become a first-class pack. The real investor artifact is not a generic summary; it is ARR/RPO/cRPO/NRR/SBC/FCF/GMV/backlog definitions with chunk IDs, comparability warnings, and confidence levels. [metric-definition-table.csv; kpi-disclosure-table.csv; source-ledger.csv]

## Evidence Gaps

No deep-research supplement was used for company operating history because the EdgarPack-derived filing bundle was good enough for that job. External research was used only where SEC filings are the wrong source: live AI price cards, credit allowances, and market-regime context. Those external rows are isolated in `ai-packaging-table.csv`, `valuation-context-table.csv`, and `raw/external/`. [ai-packaging-table.csv; valuation-context-table.csv; raw/external/ai-packaging-sources.md; raw/external/valuation-context-sources.md]

The remaining filing-side gaps are not narrative gaps; they are product gaps. Some rows still use section-level locators rather than true chunk IDs, especially where `which` did not surface the KPI or where raw filing fallback was needed. That is acceptable for this research bundle because the row is labeled medium confidence or fallback evidence, but it is not good enough for an investor-grade product promise. [kpi-disclosure-table.csv:HUBS-latest_disclosed-average-subscription-revenue-per-customer; business-model-table.csv:DDOG-current-model; business-model-table.csv:HUBS-current-model; metric-definition-table.csv:DDOG-free-cash-flow-definition]

## Bottom Line

SaaS did not simply mature from growth to profitability. It fragmented into distinct monetization machines: backlog suites, ARR suites, usage platforms, transaction platforms, SMB/customer platforms, and now AI-credit hybrids. The public filings already contain enough evidence to distinguish those machines if the tooling can reliably find the right filings, pull the right definitions, and refuse to compare metrics without provenance. This is the strongest product lesson from the exercise: EdgarPack should become less of a filing CLI and more of an investor evidence compiler that makes unsupported cross-company comparisons hard to write.
